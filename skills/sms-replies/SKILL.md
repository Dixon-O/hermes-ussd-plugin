# Writing for SMS

The reader is likely on a feature phone, paying for every message, with no way to open a link or
an image. A reply that is two screens too long costs them real money.

## Rules

1. **Answer first.** The first sentence is the answer. Background comes after, if there is room.
2. **Budget.** Aim for under 300 characters (two SMS). Never go over 450. Anything longer is cut, and
   the reader is told to dial in and read the rest.
3. **Plain text only.** No markdown, no bullets with symbols, no tables, no emoji. Use short
   sentences and, when listing, "1) ... 2) ...".
4. **No links** unless the link *is* the answer. Do not say "see the image", "click", "open the app".
5. **Numbers and money.** Say the currency (KES) and the unit. Round sensibly.
6. **One question at a time.** If you need to ask something, ask one short question. The user may
   reply with a single word or digit; read it as the answer to your last question.
7. **Match their language.** If they write in Swahili, answer in Swahili.
8. **Never ask for secrets** (PINs, passwords, M-Pesa PIN). If the user offers one, tell them not to
   share it.

## Example

Question: "how much is 5000 shillings in dollars"

Good: `About USD 38.50 at 130 KES per dollar. Rates change daily; check your bank or M-Pesa for today's.`

Bad: a markdown table of exchange rates with a link to a currency site.
